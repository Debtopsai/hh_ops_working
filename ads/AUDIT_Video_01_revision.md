## What works — keep these

1. **The count-up hook.** $53 → $50,000 in 2.5 seconds earns the first three seconds honestly. It is also the account's best-performing angle: "50k equipment" is the cheapest lead source at **$7.41 CPL** vs a $9.52 blended average.
2. **Silent-legible.** The whole message lands with sound off, which is how most of the feed watches. Nothing depends on the audio.
3. **The category stack.** Four real machines in four panels is more persuasive than any adjective, and it reads in under a second per panel.
4. **Real gear, real brands.** Turbofan, Starline, Blue Seal are all visible and all real catalogue brands. Nothing invented.
5. **Objection-handling at the end.** "Fully serviced" then "with warranty" pre-empts the second-hand doubt right before the ask.
6. **Six beats in 15 seconds** — roughly 2.4s each. That cadence holds attention without feeling frantic.

## What's wrong — ranked

### 1. No "+ GST" anywhere — fix before next spend
"$14.99/DAY" and "$50,000" both appear with no GST qualifier. Every payment or price figure in HireHospo creative is supposed to carry "+ GST"; this is NZ Fair Trading discipline, not a house preference. **Fix:** a `+ GST` tag set alongside the value line, roughly 40% of its size.

### 2. No "Subject to credit approval"
Nothing on screen says the finance is conditional. An ad that shows a daily price and a funding ceiling but never qualifies approval is the single most likely thing to draw a complaint. **Fix:** a footnote strip on the end card — `+ GST · Subject to credit approval` — small, white at ~60% opacity, above the button.

### 3. "$14.99/DAY" is the fourth daily figure in circulation
| Source | Figure |
|---|---|
| Ad-factory brand reference | $4.66/day |
| Live ad name, account 2139666836427566 | $3.99/day (A) |
| Live ad name, same account | $6.99/day (B) |
| **This video** | **$14.99/day** |

Four numbers, one offer. Whichever is correct, three are wrong, and the spread ($3.99 to $14.99) is wide enough that a customer who saw two of these ads would notice. This needs one owner and one answer before any of them run again.

### 4. Washpro is never named
"FULLY SERVICED" appears with no attribution. The house rule is that HireHospo finances and Washpro sources, delivers, installs and services. As written, the ad implies HireHospo services equipment. **Fix:** "SERVICED BY WASHPRO" — same beat, same length, correct role.

### 5. The approval-speed proof is missing
"Approved in 24 to 48 hours" is an approved claim and it is absent, while the account's data says the *speed-as-hook* ad ("Quick Process") drew **0 leads on 1,178 impressions**. Speed fails as a hook but is a strong closing reassurance. Put it on the end card, not at the front.

### 6. "Fryers" is title case; the other three panels are caps
A one-word typo-level inconsistency in the most-watched beat of the ad.

### 7. The CTA is a lead magnet, and the rest of the ad doesn't set it up
The ad sells finance for six beats, then asks for a stock-list download. That is a legitimate, probably deliberate top-of-funnel play — and the account's leadgen numbers suggest it works — but nothing in beats 1–6 tells the viewer a list exists. One word earlier in the ad ("the range", "in stock now") would make the ask feel like the natural next step rather than a swerve.

### 8. Canvas is 1906px tall, not 1920
Fourteen pixels short of the 9:16 standard. Harmless, but it means the master isn't a clean 1080×1920 and every future export inherits the odd number. Re-master at 1080×1920.

## The one-line verdict

The structure is sound and the hook is the right one — this is a good ad with a compliance problem, not a bad ad. Fix the GST tag, the credit-approval footnote, the Washpro attribution and the daily-rate conflict, and the same edit can keep running.
