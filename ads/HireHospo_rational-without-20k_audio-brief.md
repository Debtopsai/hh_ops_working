# Audio brief - HireHospo "You Can Afford The Oven" - 20s

## Music brief

- **Genre/feel:** warm, capable, mid-tempo - the most restrained of the three ads. Muted electric bass, sparse brushed percussion, a low warm pad. This ad talks to an operator with money, so the music must never sound like it is trying to impress them. **No vocals, no EDM drop, no corporate-inspirational piano.**
- **BPM 92**, steady, no tempo change. The slowest of the set. Feel: a quiet office above a restaurant, after close, going through the numbers.
- **Key:** minor through the capital-hit beats, resolving to relative major at the bridge and staying there. The resolution should read as *settled*, not triumphant - this ad ends in agreement, not excitement.
- **Energy curve by beat:**
  | Time | Beat | Energy | Direction |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 0:00-0:02 | "You can afford the oven." | 2/10 | Bass and pad only. **Do not score the line.** The viewer needs to hear a sentence, not a mood. |
  | 0:02-0:04 | "That's not the problem." | 3/10 | A single low sub hit lands with the flame line. Nothing else changes. |
  | 0:04-0:07.5 | Capital hit + runway | 4/10 → 3/10 | Percussion enters with the ledger ticks, then **decays** as the runway bar contracts. Energy falls as the bar falls. |
  | 0:07.5-0:09 | The pause | **1/10** | **Near-total drop.** Low sustain only. 1.5s of almost nothing - hold your nerve here, this is the most important silence in the set. |
  | 0:09-0:11 | Bridge | 6/10 | Full arrangement returns on the wordmark. The lift is the arrangement re-entering, not a volume push. |
  | 0:11-0:15.5 | Proof 1 + 2 | 6/10 | Hold steady and unhurried. |
  | 0:15.5-0:17.5 | Capital ledger | 6/10 | A warm pad swells very slightly under the two ticks. This is the emotional peak and it is a **small** one. |
  | 0:17.5-0:20 | End card | 5/10 → resolve | Settle and resolve, 1.5s tail past the last frame for platform trim. |
- **Search terms for the library:** "restrained warm bass 92bpm", "understated late-night business bed", "calm analogue minimal groove". Avoid uplifting, inspiring, motivational, epic, cinematic, and anything with a build-and-drop shape.

## VO direction - credit-led operator

- **Voice:** clear, confident, structured. **NZ accent.** Mid-range, warm, unhurried. **~2.25 wps.** Of the three ads this one is spoken to the most financially literate listener - the read should assume competence, not explain to it.
- **Character note for the booth:** you are talking to someone who has already run the numbers and reached the same conclusion you have. You are not informing them. You are confirming something they already suspect and giving it a name. There is no persuasion in this read at all - if it sounds like you are making a case, it is wrong.
- **Zero hype, zero upsell energy.** No rising terminals. Clean breath cuts, trimmed to −30dB or lower.

| Time | Line | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:02 | "You can afford the oven." | **Delivered as a concession, not a compliment.** Flat, matter-of-fact, slightly warm. You are conceding a point before anyone has argued it. Full stop, then a real **0.5s pause** - the pause is the pattern interrupt, not the words. |
| 0:02-0:04 | "That's not the problem." | Lower and quieter than the first line, not louder. Small stress on **"that's"**. The instinct will be to punch this line; resist it. Underplaying it is what makes it feel true. |
| 0:04-0:09 | "The problem is $20,000 leaving your account, and the six months after that." | Even and unhurried - the longest line in the ad and it must not accelerate. Small stress on **"leaving"**. **A real comma pause before "and the six months after that"**, then deliver that clause more quietly, almost as an afterthought. That clause is the actual argument of the ad. |
| 0:09-0:11 | "It's on our stock list." | The only line that gains warmth. Stress **"stock list"**. Steady, no lift at the end. The brand is on screen, not in this line; do not add it back. |
| 0:11-0:14 | "A refurbished Rational combi. With warranty." | **The single most important read in the ad.** "Refurbished" and "Rational" must carry equal confidence - a refurbished premium brand is the whole proposition, and any hesitation on "refurbished" turns it into an apology. Full stop before "With warranty", delivered as a closed statement, not a reassurance. |
| 0:14-0:16.5 | "Approved in 24 to 48 hours." | Plain and procedural. This is a process fact. No warmth needed. |
| 0:16.5-0:20 | "Get our latest stock list today." | Calm and even, six words across three and a half seconds - the most unhurried line in the set. Small stress on **"stock list"**. Full stop, no rise, no smile. "+ GST" is burned in on the end card rather than spoken. |

- Record **3 reads of the two hook lines** (flat / half a degree warmer / with a longer pause between them) and **2 reads of the Rational line** - the confidence on "refurbished" is worth having options for.
- **Do not** record any alternate take containing a weekly or daily figure. There is no version of this ad that carries one.

| 0:11 | Single dish-rack clack + a low oven-door thunk | Kitchen texture, used **once**, and the door thunk is this ad's signature sound - heavy, expensive, well-made | −16dB (clack), −15dB (thunk) |

| 0:15.8 / 0:16.2 | Two soft approval ticks, 400ms apart | The capital-preservation ledger - both sides winning, heard as two separate confirmations | −16dB |

**SFX discipline:** one rack clack, one oven-door thunk, one ambience bed. The oven-door thunk is the only cue in the whole set that is allowed to sound *expensive* - it is doing the work of communicating premium build quality without a single adjective.

## Ducking & loudness

- Music **−6 to −9 dB under VO**; the hook (0:00-0:04) rides unducked because the bed is already at 2/10 and the VO is sparse.
- **VO is the priority track.** Sidechain the bed off the VO, 40ms attack / 220ms release. The long line at 0:04-0:09 needs the gentlest release setting in the set - it should not pump.
- **Master: −14 LUFS integrated, −1.0 dBTP.**
- **Silent-read requirement:** the ad must carry fully with sound off. The hook is typographic, the agitation is a contracting bar, the promise is two ticked ledger lines - all three read silent. Burned-in captions plus on-screen text deliver every claim, including `+ GST` and `SUBJECT TO CREDIT APPROVAL`.
