## ⚠ The one thing to confirm before this runs

**The strike-through makes a promise the two other formats don't.** Crossing out $30,000 and replacing it with $11.99/day reads as *"this oven, for $11.99 a day."* The word "FROM" does not undo that — the viewer has just watched one number become the other.

So the two figures must describe **the same machine**. If $11.99/day is the entry-level oven and $30,000 is a top-of-range combi, the ad is comparing the ceiling to the floor and implying they're the same deal.

Two clean ways to resolve it, both stronger than the current pairing:

1. **Match them.** Use the outright price of the machine the $11.99/day actually finances. Then it's a true like-for-like: don't pay $X, pay $11.99/day.
2. **Make "FROM" carry real weight.** Strike a *range* — `$4,195–$30,000` — so the daily rate plainly attaches to the bottom of it.

Also worth noting: **$11.99/day is now the fifth daily rate in circulation** ($4.66 in the brand reference, $3.99 and $6.99 running live, $14.99 in Video_01). Your call which is right — but they can't all run at once.

**Catalogue check:** $30,000 is effectively real. The Rational CMP201 Electric CombiMaster 20 Tray and the Convotherm OES 12.20 24 Tray Electric are both **$29,995**. Use either as the on-screen machine and the number is honest.
