## 1. Teardown - what the case study does, and where it loses

The reference ad is a single static comparison card with no audio: "Why pay upfront?", two identical
espresso machines side by side, Purchase Outright $24,000 with three crosses versus Rent-Try-Buy
$44/day with three ticks, and a dense legal footer. Seven specific weaknesses, each of which is an
opening:

| # | Weakness | Why it costs them |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Silent** | No VO, no SFX, no music. In a feed where sound-on viewing is a third of impressions, they are voluntarily giving up a channel. |
| 2 | **Static** | Nothing moves, so there is no reason to watch past the first read. Hook rate is capped at whatever the still earns in 1.5 seconds. |
| 3 | **Both columns land at once** | The viewer reads it like a table and leaves. No sequencing means no reveal, and no reveal means no hold. |
| 4 | **The same photo twice** | Two identical machines. Visually lazy, and it wastes the strongest available argument (see move 3 below). |
| 5 | **Transparently rigged** | Three crosses against three ticks, with nothing conceded. A viewer discounts the whole card the moment they notice. |
| 6 | **"Why pay upfront?" is a weak hook** | A question with no implied answer. The viewer waits instead of engaging. |
| 7 | **$24,000 versus $44/day** | A total compared against a rate. Apples to oranges, and under NZ Fair Trading the pairing invites scrutiny. |

---

## 2. The six moves that beat it

**Move 1 - Sequence the comparison instead of presenting it.**
Show the buy-outright side **alone** for three full seconds. Let it sit. Then bring the finance side
in. The comparison becomes a reveal rather than a table, which is the entire difference between
something you read and something you watch.

**Move 2 - Make the number move.**
$30,000 counts up and locks. Later the daily figure resolves beneath it. Motion on the two numbers is
the cheapest hold-rate device available and the reference ad has none of it.

**Move 3 - Turn the identical-photo weakness into the argument.**
Same oven both sides is not a flaw, it is the point, so say it out loud: **"Same oven. Two ways to
pay."** That single line does more comparison work than six bullet points.

**Move 4 - Add sound, because they have none.**
VO plus kitchen SFX plus a bed. This is the single largest and cheapest differentiator in the
category. Also caption everything, so the muted version loses nothing.

**Move 5 - Concede a point to win the argument.**
Give the buy-outright column one genuine tick: **you own it on day one.** That is true, and conceding
it makes the other three points land as analysis rather than as sales. Biggest credibility gain
available in the format.

**Move 6 - Reconcile the two numbers instead of juxtaposing them.**
Do not put "$30,000" next to "$27/day" and leave the viewer to do the maths. Show the bridge:
**36 months at $189 a week is $29,484, and the oven is yours.** That is the fact the reference ad
cannot match, and it removes the apples-to-oranges problem in move 7 of the teardown.

---

## 3. The centrepiece

> **$30,000 to buy it today. $29,484 over three years, and you still own it.**

Over a 36-month Lease-to-Own at $189/week the total is **$29,484** against a **$30,000** cash price,
with ownership transferring at end of term. If that holds after Credit checks it, the ad does not
need a single persuasive adjective - the arithmetic is the argument, and it is the sort of claim a
competitor running a silent comparison card cannot answer.

**⚠ Verify before build.** Confirm with Credit whether deposit (standard 10 weeks in advance plus 10
weeks security), any purchase-option amount, or fees change that total. If they do, use the true
figure. The structure of the ad survives either way; only the number moves.

## 4. Format spec

| | |
|---|---|
| Length | **14s** primary · 6s cutdown |
| Cut rate | **Every 1.0 to 1.5s.** Nothing dwells except the end card |
| Aspect | **9:16 (1080×1920)** primary · 4:5 and 1:1 reflows |
| Sound | **Full mix** - VO, kitchen SFX, bed at 108 BPM. Deliberate contrast with the silent reference |
| Captions | Burned in, every line, bottom-centre inside the safe zone |
| Style | Person-free motion graphics plus one real product cut-out, dark steel system |

**Pace note.** Beat the static reference on **rate of change**, not on information. Numbers slam in
rather than count up. Benefit lines fire as staggered on-screen text so the muted read is as fast as
the sound-on read.

**No arithmetic on screen.** No "36 months x $189/week = $29,484", no totals, no working. The maths
is what Sales does after credit approval. The ad talks benefits.

## 6. Column content - exact copy

Build the comparison honestly. One conceded tick on the left is deliberate.

**LEFT · Buy outright · $30,000 today**
- ✓ You own it on day one
- ✗ $30,000 out of working capital
- ✗ Locked into this model
- ✗ Service and repair risk sits with you

**RIGHT · Lease-to-Own · from $27.00/day**
- ✓ You own it at end of term
- ✓ Capital stays in the business
- ✓ Fully serviced, with warranty
- ✓ Delivered, installed and serviced by Washpro, NZ-based

**Do not copy from the reference ad:**
- ~~"Stays off your balance sheet"~~ - an accounting claim that depends on IFRS 16 and the customer's
  own circumstances. HireHospo has no basis to assert it generically.
- ~~"Change or upgrade at any time"~~ - not true of either product. The upgrade path is at **end of
  term** on Rent (12 months); Lease-to-Own ends in ownership. Claiming "any time" is a false term.

**Rate ratio, for future ads:** across the book the daily figure runs at roughly **buy price ÷ 1,111**
(SCC 101G $189.17/wk = $27.02/day on a $30,000 unit; Turbofan E31D4 $41.30/wk implies ~$6,550). So
$50,000 of equipment is about **$45.00/day**, not $14.99. Worth putting $45/day to Credit as the
headline figure for the offer-led ads.

## 8. Production requirements

1. **One product cut-out** of the Rational SCC101 on transparent background, front three-quarter,
   lit from upper left. Used twice in frame, same asset, no flipping.
2. **Two number animations** - $30,000 count-up, $27.00/day resolve.

4. **The bridge frame** - `36 MONTHS × $189/WEEK = $29,484` in JetBrains Mono, the only frame where
   arithmetic appears on screen.
5. **VO** - NZ accent, credit-led operator register, roughly 2.1 wps. Same voice as the other six ads.
6. **Audio** - kitchen bed, oven-door thunk on the hook, till tick on each number, button on the CTA.
7. **End card** - shared component from `ad/fitout-quote-shock/shared/`.

Reuses the existing hero plinth, refurb badge, split and end-card components. Two genuinely new
frames: the comparison column and the bridge.
