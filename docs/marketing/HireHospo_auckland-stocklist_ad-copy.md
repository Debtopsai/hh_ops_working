**Four reads that drive this copy:**

1. **The payment reframe is the account's engine.** "Low Weekly Payment #3" carries 75% of spend
   and 76% of leads at the best CTR in the account. Every variant below leads with the payment,
   not the process.
2. **"Quick Process" is a proven loser** — 1,178 impressions, 14 clicks, zero leads, CPC 3× the
   winner. **Do not lead with approval speed.** It belongs in the footer, if anywhere.
3. **Frequency 3.95 against a reach of 10,371 = the audience is burnt out.** The current ceiling
   is a *reach* problem, not a creative problem — CPL held at ~$10 even at freq 3.95, which is a
   strong-creative signal. Auckland-wide expansion is the right call, and the copy has to work
   **cold**, for operators who have never heard of HireHospo. Every variant self-introduces.
4. **Daily-figure hooks work but never got volume.** $3.99/day pulled a $4.12 CPL on a small
   sample. The mechanic is sound — which is exactly why the $14.99 figure needs to be right
   (§6.1).

## 2. Why "Auckland Wide" is a claim we can actually make

From the live customer book (29 accounts): **26 sit in the Auckland region.** The footprint is
genuinely metro-wide, not central-only:

- **North:** Takapuna
- **West:** Henderson, Sunnyvale, Blockhouse Bay
- **Central:** Newmarket, Parnell, Mt Roskill, One Tree Hill, Royal Oak, Onehunga, Penrose, Roslyn
- **South:** Papatoetoe, Flat Bush, Takanini, Pukekohe, Waiuku
- **East:** Beachlands

"Takapuna to Pukekohe" is a defensible, vivid way to say Auckland-wide — it names the real arc and
it does work that the phrase "Auckland Wide" can't: it tells a Papakura operator they're in the
service area. **Use the arc, not the abstraction, wherever there's room.**

## 6. Compliance flags — clear these before the ad goes live

### 6.1 ⚠ CRITICAL — "$50k equipment from $14.99 a day" cannot run as one sentence

$14.99/day is **$104.93/week**. Over a 36-month Lease-to-Own that collects **$16,369** in total —
it cannot fund $50,000 of equipment. On the live book, $50k-scale gear sits near **$365–$414/week
($52–$59/day)**; the portfolio median deal is $65/week ($9.29/day).

Read as one claim, "50k equipment from $14.99 a day" is misleading under the Fair Trading Act.
Read as two facts it's fine. **Every variant above therefore separates them:**

> ✅ "$50,000 of equipment **on our books** / **on our stocklist**. Payments **from** $14.99 a day + GST."
> ❌ "$50,000 of equipment for $14.99 a day."

**Also confirm the figure itself.** The account has already run **$3.99/day** and **$6.99/day**,
and the brand reference carries **$4.66/day**. $14.99 is 3–4× the entry point previously
advertised and above the portfolio median. Either it's a different (larger) package — in which
case say which — or it's an error. Confirm what $14.99/day actually buys before this ships, and
then align the account on one entry figure; four different dailies in market is a trust problem.

### 6.2 ⚠ "Over 3,000 happy customers" — attribute it

The HireHospo finance book is **29 accounts**. The 3,000+ figure is the **Washpro** equipment base
— kitchens supplied and serviced, not finance customers. In a finance ad, "3,000+ happy customers"
implies 3,000 finance customers and is not substantiable.

Wording used above: **"3,000+ Kiwi hospitality kitchens fitted out / supplied"** — true, and it
still does the social-proof job. If you want the number attributed harder, use
**"Backed by Washpro — 3,000+ Kiwi kitchens supplied and serviced."**

### 6.3 ⚠ "Most competitive prices in the market" — substantiation required

An unqualified market-superiority claim needs evidence on file under FTA s.13. It also cuts
against the credit-led brand voice (§7 bans discount-shop energy). It's retained above because
you asked for it — but hold documented comparison evidence, and prefer pairing it with the price
beat, which is the substantiating mechanism:

> "Most competitive prices in the market — and if you find the same unit cheaper, we'll beat it."

If evidence isn't available, the safer swap is **"Priced to beat anything comparable in the
market"** or simply leading with the price-beat alone (V4).

### 6.4 ⚠ "We'll beat the price" — publish the terms

A price-beat guarantee needs conditions stated somewhere the customer can reach: **like-for-like
model and condition · competitor quote in writing · stock currently available · at time of
enquiry**. Add these to the landing page or lead form; the ad footer carries the short form
("Price beat on like-for-like available stock").

### 6.5 Standing gates — all satisfied above

- **"+ GST"** on every price and payment mention ✅
- **"Normal lending criteria apply"** on every variant ✅ (NZ-standard alternative to "subject to
  credit approval")
- **No approval hype** — no "guaranteed", "instant", "everyone approved" ✅
- **No pressure mechanics** — no countdowns, "last chance", "hurry" ✅
- **Condition language** — "fully serviced, with warranty", never "used" or "second-hand" ✅
- **No role-blur** — where Washpro is named it's for supply and service, HireHospo for finance ✅
- **One CTA per ad** ✅

**Note on the CTA:** the brand reference locks "Apply now". The live campaign and this script both
run a **stocklist lead magnet**, which is a lower-friction ask and is currently returning ~$10
CPL. The stocklist CTA is used throughout. Worth updating the brand reference to record the
stocklist as an approved softer CTA for top-of-funnel lead-form campaigns.

## 9. Organic social — Facebook & Instagram posts

Same campaign, different job. Organic posts reach people who already follow you, plus anyone
who taps through from the ad to check whether you're real. **The grid has to corroborate the
ad** — if the paid creative says "3,000+ kitchens, Auckland wide" and the profile is empty,
the ad's credibility drains out through the profile visit.

Three mechanical differences from the paid copy:

1. **Instagram captions can't carry a clickable link.** The route is link-in-bio, or
   comment-to-DM.
2. **Comment-to-DM ("comment STOCKLIST") is the better play** — comments are an engagement
   signal, so the post earns reach while it collects leads. It needs either a DM automation
   (ManyChat or similar) or someone replying manually. If neither exists, use "send us a
   message" instead; a comment prompt you don't answer is worse than no prompt.
3. **Truncation points differ.** Instagram cuts at ~125 characters before "…more"; Facebook
   shows roughly 3 lines on mobile. Both are front-loaded below.

**Compliance does not change for organic.** The Fair Trading Act doesn't care whether a claim
was paid for. "+ GST" and "Normal lending criteria apply" stay on every post, and the $50k /
$14.99 separation (§6.1) holds exactly as it does in the ads.
