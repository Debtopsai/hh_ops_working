# The HireHospo Call Scorecard

Eight scored dimensions, each /5, weighted to a total out of 100. Four compliance gates sit
**outside** the score as pass/fail — a breach is reported as a breach and never averaged away.

Score the call that happened, against what was achievable with *this* lead. A stock-list enquiry
turned into an agreed credit check is a strong call. A hot referral that reached the same point is
a weak one.

---

## Compliance gates — pass / fail, reported separately

Each is binary. Any FAIL leads the debrief, ahead of the score.

| Gate | Fails when | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **G1 · Credit before quote** | Any weekly figure, total, or "roughly around $X" spoken before Checkmate approval | The golden rule. Quoting pre-approval creates an expectation the credit decision may not support, and anchors the customer against the real number |
| **G2 · + GST** | A weekly payment stated without "plus GST" | NZ finance advertising. Every quoted figure is ex-GST and must be said so |
| **G3 · No approval hype** | "Guaranteed approval", "everyone gets approved", "I'll get you approved", "that's a formality" | Representing a credit outcome that has not been made. The safe frame is *subject to credit approval* |
| **G4 · No delivery promise** | A specific delivery date or "we'll have it to you by [day]" before deposit clears | Washpro fulfils 1–3 business days **after deposit clears**. HireHospo wears the blame for a missed date it never controlled |

A near-miss is still a pass, but note it. "About two-fifty a week, plus GST, subject to approval"
passes G2 and G3 — but if credit hasn't run, G1 has still failed.

## The eight dimensions

### 1 · Opening & framing — weight 10

Does the opener match **what the lead actually did** (asked for the stock list) rather than what
the seller wishes they'd done (applied for finance)?

| | |
|---|---|
| **5** | Names the stock list, confirms whether it arrived, gives a reason for the call the lead would agree with, and earns permission to ask questions. Lead is oriented within 20 seconds |
| **4** | Correct framing, slightly slow or over-explained |
| **3** | Generic but harmless — "calling about your enquiry with HireHospo" |
| **2** | Over-claims intent: treats a stock-list request as a finance enquiry. Lead audibly corrects or hesitates. Also a 2 for the opposite error — treating a named-equipment enquiry as an idle browse |
| **1** | No frame at all — straight into pitch or interrogation |

The tell for a 2: the lead says some version of *"I only wanted to have a look"* in the first
minute. That is the opener's fault, not the lead's.

### 2 · Qualification — weight 20

The highest-weighted dimension, because everything downstream depends on it. Six facts decide
whether this lead can be financed and on what product:

1. **Business type** — cafe / restaurant / caterer / cloud kitchen / food truck / bakery / bar
2. **Trading history** — how long open. The single fact that decides Rent vs Lease-to-Own
3. **Equipment need** — what and why now. The form answer is a hint, not the answer
4. **Timeframe** — opening date, current equipment failing, a lease ending
5. **Decision maker** — sole operator, partners, a spouse, a landlord
6. **Deposit capacity** — can they cover ~20 weeks upfront, or do they need an alternative tier

| | |
|---|---|
| **5** | All six established conversationally, without it feeling like a form |
| **4** | Five of six; the gap is minor |
| **3** | Three or four. Enough to proceed, not enough to tailor |
| **2** | Two or fewer. The seller is guessing at the product |
| **1** | None. The call was a pitch with a lead attached |

Trading history missing is an automatic cap of **3** on this dimension, regardless of the rest —
without it the product recommendation is a coin flip.

### 3 · Discovery depth — weight 15

Qualification gets the facts; discovery gets the *reason*. An operator asking about a dishwasher
has a problem behind it — staff spending two hours a night hand-washing, a health inspection, a
machine that died mid-service.

| | |
|---|---|
| **5** | The business problem behind the equipment is named, with a consequence and ideally a cost. The seller can now sell an outcome |
| **4** | Problem surfaced, consequence not developed |
| **3** | One "why" asked, answer accepted at face value |
| **2** | Equipment discussed as a shopping list |
| **1** | No question about the business at all |

Listen for the second "why". Most calls stop at the first answer, which is almost always the
surface one.

### 4 · Credit-first discipline — weight 15

Distinct from gate G1, which only asks *did he quote early*. This asks: **did he run the credit
conversation well?** Positioning the credit check as a normal, fast, low-friction step is a skill —
done badly it reads as a rejection risk and the lead disengages.

| | |
|---|---|
| **5** | Credit check framed as the fast, free, obvious next step ("three-minute application, 24–48 hours, then I can show you real numbers"). Lead agrees without anxiety |
| **4** | Positioned well, some hesitancy left unhandled |
| **3** | Mentioned as a hurdle rather than a step |
| **2** | Raised late or apologetically, as if it were bad news |
| **1** | Never raised — the call has no path to a quote |

The strongest version makes credit the *thing the customer wants*, because it is what unlocks the
real number: *"I can't give you a figure I might have to walk back. Let's get you checked and then
I'll show you exactly what it looks like."*

### 5 · Finance framing — weight 15

Payment not price. Correct product for the trading history. Refurbishment as the offer.

| | |
|---|---|
| **5** | Weekly payment leads, product matches trading history, refurbishment framed as the mechanism that makes premium brands affordable with warranty, deposit structure explained plainly |
| **4** | Payment-led and correct product, refurbishment handled neutrally |
| **3** | Slips into total price at least once, or is vague on deposit |
| **2** | Leads with equipment price; or offers the wrong product for the trading history |
| **1** | Apologises for refurbishment, or cannot explain the deposit when asked |

Automatic **2** or below if the wrong product was offered — recommending 36-month Lease-to-Own to
a business trading six weeks is not a framing slip, it is a listening failure.

### 6 · Objection handling — weight 10

Score the *worst-handled* objection, not the average. See `objections.md`.

| | |
|---|---|
| **5** | Acknowledged, isolated ("is that the only thing?"), answered on the customer's terms, confirmed as resolved |
| **4** | Answered well, not confirmed resolved |
| **3** | Answered with a fact, no acknowledgement — technically correct, felt like a rebuttal |
| **2** | Talked over, or answered a different objection than the one raised |
| **1** | Conceded, or ignored it entirely |

An objection that recurs later in the call was never resolved the first time — score the first
attempt as a 2 at best.

### 7 · Next step — weight 10

| | |
|---|---|
| **5** | Specific, dated, mutual, with the lead's own commitment stated back ("so I'll send the application now, you'll do it tonight, and I'll call Thursday at 10") |
| **4** | Specific and dated, one-sided |
| **3** | Agreed in principle, no date — "I'll send it through and follow up" |
| **2** | Left with the customer — "have a think and let me know" |
| **1** | Call ends with nothing |

"I'll email you the information" with no date is a **2**. It feels polite and closes nothing.

### 8 · Control & listening — weight 5

Talk ratio, interruptions, silence tolerated after a question, whether the seller heard the thing
the lead said quietly at 3:40 and never returned to.

| | |
|---|---|
| **5** | Lead talks more than the seller in the discovery half; questions land and are left to breathe |
| **3** | Balanced, some talking over |
| **1** | Seller monologues; lead gives one-word answers |

The most common finding here: the lead reveals something important — a partner who decides, a
lease ending, a failed machine — and the seller acknowledges it and moves on without exploring it.

## Scoring

```
Total = (D1×2) + (D2×4) + (D3×3) + (D4×3) + (D5×3) + (D6×2) + (D7×2) + (D8×1)
```
Maximum 100.

| Band | Reading |
|---|---|
| **85–100** | Strong. Repeatable. Extract what worked and name it so it can be reused |
| **70–84** | Solid, one clear gap. The debrief has an obvious single theme |
| **55–69** | Functional but leaking. Usually qualification or next step |
| **40–54** | The call cost money. Lead is probably recoverable with a good follow-up |
| **<40** | Lead likely lost. Coach the pattern, not the call |

**Any compliance FAIL is reported above the band and is never offset by a high score.** A 91 with
a G1 breach is a 91 with a breach, and the breach leads the debrief.
