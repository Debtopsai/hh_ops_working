## First, the important bit: this is commercial equipment

Commercial kitchen equipment is built to work hard, but it is not a domestic
appliance and it does not look after itself.

- Run eight hours a day, one of these machines does in a day what a home unit
  would take a week to do. Heat, water, grease and constant use wear parts fast.
- Your warranty covers genuine faults in the equipment. It does not cover damage
  caused by neglect, such as scale, blocked coils, old oil or the wrong
  chemicals. Good care keeps you out of that gap.
- Downtime costs more than maintenance, every time. A dishwasher that dies on a
  Friday night or a fridge that fails overnight costs you a call-out, a repair
  and lost trade. Prevention is always cheaper.

None of this is meant to worry you. A few minutes of care each day and a
professional service on schedule will keep your kitchen running.

## The five rules that prevent most breakdowns

**1. Water is the number one enemy.** Scale from the water supply builds up on
heating elements and inside boilers, and it is the single most common cause of
failure in dishwashers, glasswashers, combi ovens, ice makers and coffee
machines. Even New Zealand's soft to moderately hard water will scale a hot
commercial machine over time. Find out how hard your local water is, fit
filtration or softening on any machine that heats water, and descale on schedule.

**2. Keep it clean, every day.** Grease, food debris and blocked filters are
behind a huge share of faults. A daily clean is the cheapest maintenance there is.

**3. Give it air.** Machines need airflow to shed heat. This matters most for
fridges and freezers: keep the condenser coil clean, leave space around the unit,
and keep it out of direct heat. A blocked coil is the fastest way to kill a
compressor.

**4. Use it the way it is meant to be used.** Use the correct chemicals and oil,
do not overload it, allow warm-up and cool-down where needed, and make sure your
team knows how to run it. Most misuse is simply nobody being shown.

**5. Service it on schedule, and report problems early.** A small noise, leak or
drop in performance is a cheap fix today. Ignored, it becomes a breakdown. Book a
professional service on a regular cycle, and call us the moment something is not
right.

## How often: your maintenance rhythm

The heavy-use rule: **if a machine runs around eight hours a day, it needs a
professional service about four times a year, once a quarter.** Lighter use, once
or twice a year is usually enough. Water-fed machines also need filter changes and
descaling in between, set to your local water hardness.

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Every day** | Wipe down. Empty and clean filters, traps and drip trays. Skim the fryer. Clear dishwasher spray arms and jets. Scrape the griddle. Check door seals. Leave washers open to dry. |
| **Every week** | Deeper clean, including behind and underneath. Descale checks on water-fed gear. Boil-out the fryer on busy sites. Sanitise ice makers and prep equipment. |
| **Every month** | Descale water-fed machines. Clean refrigeration condenser coils. Check gaskets and seals. Fryer boil-out. Change or check water filters. |
| **Every quarter** | **Book one professional maintenance session.** This is the four-a-year cadence for equipment used around eight hours a day. It covers calibration, gas and electrical checks, and water filter changes. |
| **Every year** | For lighter-use sites, one to two professional services a year is usually enough. Gas safety check on all gas equipment. |

## Machine by machine

Quick care notes for the main categories. Always follow the manufacturer's manual
for your specific model as well.

### Dishwashers and glasswashers
- **Daily:** empty and rinse the filters, clear the wash and rinse arms so no jet
  is blocked, wipe the seals, and leave the door open to air-dry.
- **Regular:** descale to suit your water hardness, check detergent and rinse-aid
  dosing, and clean the tank.
- **What kills them:** limescale in the boiler and blocked jets. Soft water still
  scales a hot machine over time.
- **Call us if:** wash temperatures drop, glasses come out cloudy, or you can see
  scale building up.

### Combi and convection ovens
- **Daily:** run the cleaning cycle, wipe the cavity, clean the door seal, and
  check the drain is clear.
- **Regular:** descale the steam generator (monthly is a common guide, more often
  in hard water), keep the water filter in date, and never ignore a descale alert.
- **What kills them:** scale in the steam generator and neglected door seals.
  Water treatment is essential on a combi oven.
- **Convection ovens:** keep the fan and interior clean and check the door seal and
  hinges.

### Ranges, cooktops and gas burners
- **Daily:** clean the burners and drip trays and keep the burner ports clear.
- **Regular:** check ignition and the flame (it should burn blue), clear any
  blocked jets, and keep grease down.
- **Gas is Washpro's job:** never modify gas connections yourself. Book us for any
  gas work and an annual gas safety check.

### Griddles, grills and salamanders
- **Daily:** scrape and clean the plate, empty the grease trays, and season the
  surface where the manual calls for it.
- **Regular:** clear carbon build-up and check the elements or burners.
- **What kills them:** built-up carbon and grease, and running them dry.

### Deep fryers and pasta cookers
- **Daily:** skim, filter the oil, wipe down and clean the baskets.
- **Regular:** change the oil on your schedule and boil-out the vat (about monthly,
  weekly for busy sites). Check the thermostat and high-limit cut-out.
- **What kills them:** old, dirty oil and carbon on the elements. Never overfill,
  and never leave a fryer on and unattended.

### Pizza and conveyor ovens
- **Daily and weekly:** clean crumb trays, stones and conveyor belts, and check the
  heating elements.
- Keep the ventilation around the oven clear.

### Bakery and dough equipment (mixers, sheeters, provers)
- **After each use:** clean bowls, beaters, rollers and guards.
- **Regular:** check the drive belts and gears, keep it lubricated where the manual
  specifies, and never exceed the bowl or dough capacity. Overloading is the fastest
  way to burn out a motor or gearbox.

### Refrigeration and ice
- **The big one:** clean the condenser coil. A coil clogged with dust and grease is
  the top cause of compressor failure. Clean it monthly in a greasy kitchen, every
  three months in a clean one.
- **Daily and weekly:** check and clean the door seals, do not block the airflow
  inside or around the unit, and keep it out of direct heat.
- **Ice makers:** descale and sanitise on schedule. Scale and slime build up fast.
- **What kills them:** blocked coils and worn seals. A dirty coil also pushes your
  power bill up by 20 to 30 percent.

### Holding, display and food warming
- Clean the water pans, elements and seals, check the thermostat, and keep the unit
  clean and dry.

### Food prep and slicing
- Clean and sanitise after every use, handle and store blades safely, check the
  guards, and lubricate where the manual specifies.

## Your kitchen matters as much as the machine

A well-maintained machine in a poor environment will still struggle. Run through
this checklist for your kitchen:

- **Ventilation and extraction** working and clean. Grease-laden air ruins
  electronics and coils.
- **Space around units** for airflow, especially fridges and freezers. Do not push
  them hard against a wall or box them in.
- **Ambient heat.** Fridges and freezers work harder in a hot kitchen. Keep them
  away from ovens and direct sun where you can.
- **Water.** Know your hardness and fit filtration or softening on any water-fed
  equipment.
- **Cleanliness.** Keep floors, surfaces and the backs of machines free of grease
  and dust, and stay on top of pest control.
- **Stable power.** Report any trips or flickering to us.

## What your warranty covers, and what it does not

- **Covered:** genuine faults in the equipment.
- **Not covered:** damage from neglect or misuse. That includes scale build-up,
  blocked condenser coils, old oil, the wrong chemicals, overloading, running a
  machine dry, and any unapproved modifications.

This is not about catching you out. These failures are preventable, and this guide
is how you prevent them. Keeping up with care, and keeping a simple record of it,
protects both your equipment and your warranty.
