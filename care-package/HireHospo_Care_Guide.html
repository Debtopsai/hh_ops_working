Looking after your equipment

A care guide for your commercial kitchen

Congratulations on your new equipment. This guide is here to help you get years of reliable service out of it. Please take ten minutes to read it, and keep it somewhere your team can find it.

Most of the breakdowns we see are not the machine giving up. They are the result of equipment that was run hard and never cared for. Nearly all of it is preventable, and this guide shows you how.

A quick note on who does what. HireHospo finances your equipment. Washpro delivers, installs and services it. The day-to-day care is the one part that sits with you, and it is the part that makes the biggest difference.

First, the important bit: this is commercial equipment

Commercial kitchen equipment is built to work hard, but it is not a domestic appliance and it does not look after itself.

  • Run eight hours a day, one of these machines does in a day what a home unit would take a week to do. Heat, water, grease and constant use wear parts fast.
  • Your warranty covers genuine faults in the equipment. It does not cover damage caused by neglect, such as scale, blocked coils, old oil or the wrong chemicals. Good care keeps you out of that gap.
  • Downtime costs more than maintenance, every time. A dishwasher that dies on a Friday night or a fridge that fails overnight costs you a call-out, a repair and lost trade. Prevention is always cheaper.

None of this is meant to worry you. A few minutes of care each day and a professional service on schedule will keep your kitchen running.

The five rules that prevent most breakdowns

1

Water is the number one enemy

Scale from the water supply builds up on heating elements and inside boilers, and it is the single most common cause of failure in dishwashers, glasswashers, combi ovens, ice makers and coffee machines. Even New Zealand's soft to moderately hard water will scale a hot commercial machine over time. Find out how hard your local water is, fit filtration or softening on any machine that heats water, and descale on schedule.

2

Keep it clean, every day

Grease, food debris and blocked filters are behind a huge share of faults. A daily clean is the cheapest maintenance there is.

3

Give it air

Machines need airflow to shed heat. This matters most for fridges and freezers: keep the condenser coil clean, leave space around the unit, and keep it out of direct heat. A blocked coil is the fastest way to kill a compressor.

4

Use it the way it is meant to be used

Use the correct chemicals and oil, do not overload it, allow warm-up and cool-down where needed, and make sure your team knows how to run it. Most misuse is simply nobody being shown.

5

Service it on schedule, and report problems early

A small noise, leak or drop in performance is a cheap fix today. Ignored, it becomes a breakdown. Book a professional service on a regular cycle, and call us the moment something is not right.

How often: your maintenance rhythm

The heavy-use rule: if a machine runs around eight hours a day, it needs a professional service about four times a year, once a quarter. Lighter use, once or twice a year is usually enough. Water-fed machines also need filter changes and descaling in between, set to your local water hardness.

Machine by machine

Quick care notes for the main categories. Always follow the manufacturer's manual for your specific model as well.

Dishwashers and glasswashers

  • Daily: empty and rinse the filters, clear the wash and rinse arms so no jet is blocked, wipe the seals, leave the door open to dry.
  • Regular: descale to suit your water hardness, check detergent and rinse-aid dosing, clean the tank.
  • What kills them: limescale in the boiler and blocked jets. Soft water still scales a hot machine over time.

Combi and convection ovens

  • Daily: run the cleaning cycle, wipe the cavity, clean the door seal, check the drain is clear.
  • Regular: descale the steam generator (monthly is a common guide, more in hard water), keep the water filter in date, never ignore a descale alert.
  • What kills them: scale in the steam generator and neglected door seals. Water treatment is essential on a combi.

Ranges, cooktops and gas burners

  • Daily: clean the burners and drip trays, keep the burner ports clear.
  • Regular: check ignition and the flame (it should burn blue), clear blocked jets, keep grease down.
  • Gas is Washpro's job: never modify gas connections yourself. Book us for gas work and an annual gas safety check.

Griddles, grills and salamanders

  • Daily: scrape and clean the plate, empty the grease trays, season the surface where the manual calls for it.
  • Regular: clear carbon build-up, check the elements or burners.
  • What kills them: built-up carbon and grease, and running them dry.

Deep fryers and pasta cookers

  • Daily: skim, filter the oil, wipe down, clean the baskets.
  • Regular: change the oil on your schedule and boil-out the vat (about monthly, weekly for busy sites). Check the thermostat and high-limit cut-out.
  • What kills them: old, dirty oil and carbon on the elements. Never overfill, and never leave a fryer on and unattended.

Pizza and conveyor ovens

  • Daily and weekly: clean crumb trays, stones and conveyor belts, check the heating elements.
  • Keep the ventilation around the oven clear.

Bakery and dough equipment

  • After each use: clean bowls, beaters, rollers and guards.
  • Regular: check drive belts and gears, keep it lubricated where specified.
  • What kills them: overloading past the bowl or dough capacity. It is the fastest way to burn out a motor or gearbox.

Refrigeration and ice

  • The big one: clean the condenser coil. A coil clogged with dust and grease is the top cause of compressor failure. Clean it monthly in a greasy kitchen, every three months in a clean one.
  • Daily and weekly: check the door seals, do not block airflow around the unit, keep it out of direct heat.
  • Ice makers: descale and sanitise on schedule. Scale and slime build up fast.
  • What kills them: blocked coils and worn seals. A dirty coil also pushes your power bill up 20 to 30 percent.

Holding, display and food warming

  • Clean the water pans, elements and seals, check the thermostat, keep the unit clean and dry.

Food prep and slicing

  • Clean and sanitise after every use, handle and store blades safely, check the guards, lubricate where the manual specifies.

Your kitchen matters as much as the machine

A well-maintained machine in a poor environment will still struggle. Run through this checklist for your kitchen:

  • Ventilation and extraction working and clean. Grease-laden air ruins electronics and coils.
  • Space around units for airflow, especially fridges and freezers. Do not push them hard against a wall or box them in.
  • Ambient heat. Fridges and freezers work harder in a hot kitchen. Keep them away from ovens and direct sun where you can.
  • Water. Know your hardness and fit filtration or softening on any water-fed equipment.
  • Cleanliness. Keep floors, surfaces and the backs of machines free of grease and dust, and stay on top of pest control.
  • Stable power. Report any trips or flickering to us.

What your warranty covers, and what it does not

Covered

  • Genuine faults in the equipment.

Not covered

  • Scale build-up
  • Blocked condenser coils
  • Old or dirty oil
  • The wrong chemicals
  • Overloading or running dry
  • Unapproved modifications

This is not about catching you out. These failures are preventable, and this guide is how you prevent them. Keeping up with care, and keeping a simple record of it, protects both your equipment and your warranty.
