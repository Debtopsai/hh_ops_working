# 06 — "Top Hospo Businesses" campaign — industry segments

*A separate campaign from the launch (`01`–`05`). The concept: take the Video 3 social-proof template — **"What do Auckland's top [industry] have in common? They don't buy their equipment outright. They finance it, and keep their cash working."** — and run it **localised per industry**, each industry in its **own ad set** targeted to it. Same brand voice, same "Get our latest stock list today" CTA, same compliance locks. Auckland, NZD, ex-GST.*

**Why this structure works (and the launch one didn't split):** here you're **segmenting by audience** — each ad set has a genuinely different target *and* a different creative (the industry swapped in). That's the legitimate reason to run multiple ad sets. The launch campaign was one audience, so splitting there just starved the budget; here the split is the point.

**The real targeting lever is the creative, not the interest.** Meta's interest targeting is weak for *owners* of a business type (interests mostly reach *consumers* of it). So the pattern per ad set is: **broad-ish targeting + one light interest signal, and let the "top [industry]" hook self-select** the operator. The person scrolling who runs a bakery stops on "Auckland's top bakeries." That headline is the targeting.

**Two segments to treat differently (weak on Meta):**
- **Aged care / childcare / institutional canteens** — real commercial kitchens (combi ovens, bratt pans, passthrough/conveyor dishwashers, holding, refrigeration), but Meta interest targeting barely reaches procurement/owners here. Better via direct/LinkedIn/email than a Meta social-proof ad. If you do run it, soften the hook — "top" social proof is weaker for institutional: *"The best-run care kitchens don't tie up their capital in equipment."*
- **Ghost/cloud kitchens** (#9) — reachable but thin audience in Auckland; expect low volume.

## How to run it (strategy)

- **One ad set per industry**, each with: Auckland targeting + the one light interest signal above + audience expansion ON, and the **matching localised creative** (Video 3 with the industry and equipment swapped). The creative self-selects the operator.
- **Don't launch all 11 at once — budget still rules.** Each ad set needs enough spend to deliver. At a lean budget, start with the **3–5 biggest, most-reachable segments** (cafés, restaurants, bakeries, bars, caterers), prove the template, then add segments as budget grows. At higher budget you can run more ad sets in parallel.
- **Priority order** (reach × equipment fit × how well "top [X]" self-selects): 1 Cafés · 2 Restaurants · 3 Bakeries · 4 Bars · 5 Caterers · 6 Takeaways · 7 Pizzerias · 8 Food trucks · then venues / hotels / delivery kitchens.
- **Creative production:** it's one template — swap the opening word ("cafés" → "restaurants" → "bakeries"…) and the equipment B-roll to match the industry. Cheap to produce a whole set from the Video 3 master.
- **Exclusions & compliance:** carry over from the launch — exclude existing customers, "+GST" only on any dollar figure, "subject to credit approval" microcopy on the end card, "outright" kept in the claim, no Washpro on screen, real catalogue equipment only. And the same ⚠ as Ad 3: *"top [industry]"* is social-proof puffery — fine, as long as you're not implying specific named venues are customers.
